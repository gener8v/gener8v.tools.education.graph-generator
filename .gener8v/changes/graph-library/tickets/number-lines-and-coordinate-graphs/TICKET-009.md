# TICKET-009: Choose a default tick spacing the drawn axis has room for

**Change:** graph-library
**Capability Area:** Number Lines & Coordinate Graphs (number-lines-and-coordinate-graphs)
**Specification:** specifications/number-lines-and-coordinate-graphs.md

**Summary:** The default tick spacing (1, 2 or 5 × 10ⁿ, giving 7 to 20 ticks) is chosen from the axis range alone, so on an x,y graph whose plot is narrowed (down to 120 units wide) or flat (about 50 to 60 units tall) the default tick labels run together. x −2..2 with y −100..100 draws nine x labels about 15 units apart ("-2-1.5-1-0.5"), and x −10..10 with y −2..2, the example in TICKET-003's own equal-units criterion, draws y labels about 8 units apart for text 12 units tall. Make the default depend on the length the axis is drawn at: the finest 1, 2 or 5 × 10ⁿ spacing, never finer than today's default, at which neighboring labels clear one another. Decide it once, so `describe`, the checks and the layout agree on it. Fixes graph-library/number-lines-and-coordinate-graphs-ticket-003-code-review/CR-002 and graph-library/number-lines-and-coordinate-graphs-ticket-003-quality-review/QR-004, deferred to this ticket by the Engineer (Thomas Eaves, 2026-10-27).
**Priority:** Must
**Value:** A curriculum developer who leaves the tick spacing out gets readable tick labels on any x,y graph, including a tall range narrowed to fit the page and a wide flat one, and the text alternative states the spacing the figure shows.

**Requirements Covered:**
- NLC-REQ-005: A documented default tick spacing when none is supplied (the documented rule gains the drawn length; see Known Hazards on the "ticks" wording)
- NLC-REQ-006: Tick labels shown by default (the bug: they are shown, but run together on a short axis)
- NLC-REQ-012: Text alternative generated from the normalized description (it states the spacing the figure draws)
- NLC-REQ-027: Separate tick spacing for the x-axis and the y-axis (each axis's default is chosen for its own length)
- NLC-REQ-032: One x unit and one y unit drawn at equal lengths by default (the narrowing and clamp of TICKET-003 DEL-002 are what make the axes short; its Known Hazard asks that labels stay legible)

**Known Hazards:**
- The text alternative states the default spacing from numbers alone. `describe` of the number line and the x,y graph states `axisSpacing(min, max, given)` as "ticked every N", and `describe(input)` runs in `./core` with no layout. The font-metrics table behind `textWidth` is in `src/layout/kit/text.ts`, which `./core` must not import. So a spacing chosen in the layout from measured labels would make the text alternative state a spacing the figure does not draw. So do: write one pure function of numbers in `src/core/value-scale.ts` and call it from `describe`, the layout and (TICKET-004) the x,y,z graph. Bound a label's width by its character count (digits are at most 0.556 em, with `TEXT_WIDTH_SAFETY`), taking the label text from `axisTickLabel(tick, spacing)`. The property test measures with `textWidth`, so an estimate below it fails there. Do not copy the font table into core.
- The drawn length is not known in core. The plot's width is the smaller of the room the layout leaves (after the widest y labels, the y-axis label's lines and half the widest x label) and 360 / ratio; its height is the width × ratio; the y-axis label is caller text. So do: decide each axis for a length the layout is certain to give. Work the room out from numbers: the widest labels at the length-unaware default spacing (a coarser spacing never prints more digits or decimals) and two lines for the y-axis label when one is present (`AXIS_LABEL_MAX_LINES`). The layout then draws a plot at least that long. Do NOT decide for the full 368 units: the real plot is narrower once the y labels take their room, and the labels would touch again. Do NOT decide for a worst-case floor of every graph (about 200 units): with the bar graph's 6-unit gap, x −10..10 would go from every 2 to every 5, which `tests/core/xy-graph.test.ts` pins. If no figure keeps ordinary graphs unchanged and protects narrow ones, stop and raise it; do not weaken a criterion.
- The y-axis is affected as much as the x-axis, and differently. Along the x-axis a label needs its width; along the y-axis it needs its height (the tick font size, `LINE_HEIGHT` for the room), and `drawAxis` puts each y label level with its tick. A flat plot (ratio down to 1/6, 50 to 60 units tall) cannot hold 7 or more y labels. So do: pass each axis its own length and its own label extent, and test both axes.
- Some ranges have no fitting spacing. The candidates are 1, 2 and 5 × 10ⁿ, up to 2.5 times apart, so on a short axis a range can have no spacing that both clears the labels and leaves two ticks. A scan of 728 ranges from 0.001 to 10⁶ wide found 47 (6%) at 120 units with a 6-unit gap, and none at 200 units; x 177.8..1956 at 120 units is one (spacing 500 gives labels 500, 1000, 1500 with the last two 33.7 units apart where they need 35, and spacing 1000 leaves one tick). On a 61-unit y-axis, 27 of 1,448 ranges had none with 14.4-unit label boxes and no gap. So do: fall back to the coarsest spacing that still leaves two ticks (fewer than two is the no-scale figure TICKET-007 rejects), say so in the function's doc comment, and have the property test count the descriptions its own search excludes. Record the count and one example in the delivery record. Labels may touch there. Thinning labels would remove those cases, but it is a larger change; raise it to the Product Owner (see Notes) rather than adding it here.
- The spec's "ticks" wording becomes false for short axes. NLC-REQ-005 says only "a documented default tick spacing". The count is stated in three places that already differ: the specification's Behaviors & Rules → Ticks ("between 5 and 20 ticks"), the published `tickSpacing` description (same, "fewer on a narrower one", meaning a narrower range), and the code, its comments and TICKET-007 ("7 to 20 ticks", from 19 intervals). A length-aware default gives fewer ticks on a short axis, down to two. So do: change the published description and the code comments here; do NOT edit `.gener8v/specifications/number-lines-and-coordinate-graphs.md`. Say in the delivery record that the Ticks rule needs an amendment through the Specification skill (Product Owner), as TICKET-007 did for its new rule, and keep `@spec NLC-REQ-005` on the code until then.
- `./core` is at 24.55 kB of 25 kB (delivery record of TICKET-003). The rule belongs in core because `describe` is there, so keep it to a few lines of arithmetic: no table, no new module, no text kit. Run `pnpm size` before and after. If `./core` passes 25 kB, do NOT raise the limit here: the budget is an open decision (quality review QR-005) and TICKET-004 also needs room. Stop and raise it to the Architect.
- NLC TICKET-006's label rule stays. A label's text, and so its width bound, comes from `axisTickLabel(tick, spacing)` (`labelDecimals`). Do not format a tick a second way, and do not round ticks or labels again in `src/layout/xy-graph.ts` or `src/layout/kit/axis.ts`.
- Existing signatures keep their results. `axisSpacing(min, max, spacing?)`, `niceTicks` and `drawableTicks` return what they return today when no length is given, because the number line, the checks and `tests/layout/kit/ticks.test.ts` read them that way. `drawableTicks` is the one place that turns a missing spacing into ticks for the checks and the layout, so the length goes in there as a new optional input. `tickCount` and `too_many_ticks` count a supplied spacing only (a default never gives more than 20 ticks), so they stay as they are. TICKET-007 (not yet delivered) names `axisSpacing(min, max)` as the spacing that fits in its `too_few_ticks` message and asserts that spacing validates. That stays true for any length, since a supplied spacing is used as given. Leave that message on the length-unaware rule; if TICKET-007 is delivered second, its tests need no change.
- Existing assertions that name a default on a colliding plot will change. `tests/core/xy-graph.test.ts:39` states "x from -10 to 10 ticked every 2, y from 0 to 100 ticked every 10": that ratio is 5, so the plot is 120 units wide and its 11 x labels are 12 units apart today. `tests/layout/xy-graph.test.ts:143` ("ticks each axis at its own spacing, or its default…") takes the expected ticks from `niceTicks(min, max, spacing)`, which for x 0..100 with y −3..3 (a flat plot) is no longer what is drawn. "leaves the origin unlabeled rather than crowd its neighbours on densely ticked axes" uses x −2..2 with y −5..5 and may no longer be densely ticked. So do: change an assertion only when the old drawing overlaps, take expected ticks from the same decision the layout uses, and list each change in the delivery record. A changed assertion on a graph with room is a regression, not an update.
- The `./core` budget is now 40 kB (graph-library/number-lines-and-coordinate-graphs-ticket-003-quality-review/QR-005, Engineer — Thomas Eaves, 2026-10-27). `./core` holds every type's definition by design (AD-001), while CPSE-NFR-002's 25 kB per-type limit is enforced on the per-type entries (`./xy-graph`, `./number-line`, …), which stay at 25 kB. **This supersedes any criterion or hazard above that holds `./core` to 25 kB.** So keep core changes small, check `./core` against 40 kB, and check this type's own entry against 25 kB.

**Notes:** Medium because the rule touches `./core`, both axes of the layout, the text alternative, the published description and the generated files, with a property test that finds its own counter-examples. The uncertain part is the room bound (second hazard); if it cannot be worked out from numbers, stop and raise it instead of sizing up. TICKET-003 is delivered and reviewed, and this ticket changes its layout, so it has no open predecessor.

The number line has the same defect for labels of about six characters or more, which this ticket does not fix: `-100000..100000` ticked every 20000 overlaps its neighbors by 12.8 units, and `0.001..0.0195` ticked every 0.001 by 15 (probe on the working tree, 2026-10-27). Its drawings in the existing tests must not change, and its `describe` and `tickLabelsOnTicks` read the length-unaware default. The shared function takes a length, so the number line can adopt it in a follow-up ticket with its own tests; raise that to the Product Owner.

Thinning labels instead (label every second or fifth tick when neighbors would touch) is the other option CR-002 named. The ticks, `describe`, the checks, the generated files and the specification's tick count would all stay as they are, `./core` would not grow, and no range would lack a fit; the cost is that fewer ticks carry a label than the grid shows. This ticket follows the Engineer's choice of a length-aware spacing. If the one-decision hazard cannot be met, raise label thinning to the Product Owner rather than switching to it silently.
